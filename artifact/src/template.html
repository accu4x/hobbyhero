    What this is, and what it isn't

    • What it is. Two Poisson regressions predict each team's goals from 19 inputs: recent expected-goal form (10- and 40-game weightings), the starting goalie's goals saved above expected, shot attempts for and against, and power-play and penalty-kill rates. The win chance comes from the grid of possible scores. Games level after overtime are split using the historical home share of overtime and shootout wins.
    • What it knew, and when. Every past game on this page was predicted by a model refit at the start of that month on earlier games only, with a shot model trained on earlier seasons only. It never saw the game it was predicting. The off-season view uses a model trained on all games through , and knows nothing about trades, signings or injuries since.
    • This season's schedule. Games not yet played show a pre-season estimate: the off-season model on each team's form at the end of last season, with the goalie who started the most of that team's regular-season games last season. It knows nothing about trades, signings, injuries or who actually starts in goal, and the estimates stay fixed until the page is rebuilt with played games.
    • The market is a yardstick, not an input. The closing moneyline is shown as a win % with the vig removed, next to the model, only to compare them. The model never sees it.
    • How good it is. Before building this page, the model was tested on two windows chosen in advance:

    Log loss rewards well-calibrated probabilities; lower is better, and 0.693 is a coin flip. Hockey is one of the most random major sports, so small differences here are real work.

    • What it isn't. Not betting advice. It has not beaten the closing market, and the page says so.
